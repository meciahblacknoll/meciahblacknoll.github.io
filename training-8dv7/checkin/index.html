Daily Check-in Layer

What a thirty-second spoken note from the bedroom gym should turn into, and what the program is allowed to say back. The three training documents already contain every rule this page uses; what they do not contain is a way for "I slept five hours" or "my right wrist is pinchy" to reach those rules. This page is that connection: the fields a note maps to, the bounded adjustments each rule permits, three worked days, and the smallest log that makes the 48-hour clock, the stall counter and the sleep week actually computable.

01What this adds, and what it leaves alone

The base document settles what you do, Block 1 settles when, and Mobility Skill Lines adds two ladders and a benchmark test. None of that is re-opened here. Not one training rule on this page is new: every adjustment in Section 04 quotes the document and section it comes from, and where the documents are silent, Section 08 says so instead of filling the silence with a plausible-sounding rule.

What this page adds is the layer the original idea was actually about. The ChatGPT conversation that started it was not about hardware; it was about a system that hears "I scraped my knee this morning" and makes an adjustment that is "not wild or crazy," decided by "whether or not it will actually slow down my goals," while you stay "the one in charge." Read against the three documents, that sentence is already almost fully specified. "Whether it slows down my goals" is the base document's priority order, with joint health winning every tie. "Not wild or crazy" is the fixed menu of moves the documents already permit: one tier down, out for the week, drop Friday, pull the deload forward, hold the tier this week. "You stay in charge" means the system proposes and never applies.

The one design rule

Log everything, look up the rule, propose the move, change nothing. The system has no judgement of its own about your body. It has a log, the three documents, and a calendar. When a note matches a rule, it says which rule and what the rule permits, in one to four lines, and ends with a choice. When a note matches nothing, it logs the note word for word and says so.

Still the override

Any new ache that lingers past 48 hours drops that movement back one tier, or out of the session entirely, for that week. This layer exists mostly to make that rule operable: the base document says the 48-hour rule "needs the date," and until now nothing was writing the date down.

Hardware is settled and not discussed here: a voice memo to the existing Telegram bot, transcribed by the local whisper pipeline, arriving as text. Everything below starts at the moment that text exists.

03The check-in: speech to fields

A check-in is whatever you say into the phone, usually once after a session and sometimes once in the morning. One note can carry several facts ("slept six, floor press one-fifteen for nine eight eight eight, wrist's a bit tender") and each fact becomes its own entry. The parser is a vocabulary lookup, not a conversation: it recognises the words the documents use for joints, lifts, ladders and tiers, pulls out the numbers, and fills the fields below. Where a required field is missing it asks one question. It never guesses a side, a date or a tier.

Aches and pain

The most important field is the one the documents care most about and the one speech is loosest about: is this the joint or the muscle? The base says the stretch "belongs in the muscle belly, never in the knee, the inside of the hip joint, or the sitting bone tendon attachment"; Mobility says "hamstring, glute and quad fatigue is fine" and names, per skill, the exact spots that are not. So the vocabulary is built from those lists, and a sore muscle is logged but starts no clock.

Skill ladders

Five ladders: planche, handstand push-up, front split, side split (both tracked by support height rather than tier), pistol squat, back bridge. The field that matters most for the tier rule is clean, defined in the base as "the last set looks like the first: locked elbows, no hip sag, no arching, no wrist pain."

Monthly and one-time measurements

Tape (eight numbers, Friday of weeks 4, 8 and 12), the longevity battery (weeks 1 and 12) and the strength benchmarks (week 2) are better typed than spoken, but the parser accepts them spoken as "name, number" pairs and reads back the whole set before saving, because a mis-heard waist measurement corrupts three ratio reads.

Anything else

If no vocabulary matches, the note is saved verbatim as type: note and the reply is one line: "Logged as a note, word for word. Nothing in the program keys off this, so no change proposed." Section 06 says more about why that is the right behaviour.

04Adjustments the system may propose

Every row below is a rule that already exists, written in the trigger form the documents use, with its source and the exact shape of what the system says. Replies are short, name the rule in plain words, propose one move, and end with yes / no / something else. Nothing is ever applied silently; "applied" here means you said yes and the log records that you did.

Reply template, every time

Logged: [what was recorded]. · Rule: [one plain sentence, with the document]. · Proposal: [one bounded move]. · Yes / no / something else? Four lines maximum. No explanation of why the rule exists; that lives in the documents.

P · Progression and stalls

"Stalled" is the one word these rules depend on that the documents never define numerically. The definition used here is in Section 08, gap 1, and it is the most literal reading available: a session at the same load where total reps did not go up is a no-progress session; two in a row is a stall. Nothing fires on one.

What is deliberately not on this list

A same-day exercise swap "from the substitution ladder" because of pain. 06What this does not do yet

This is a logging and rule-lookup layer. It has a vocabulary, a log and the three documents. It does not have a model of your body, your week outside the gym, or your goals beyond the three the base document ranks. Being specific about that boundary is what keeps it trustworthy.

  • It does not diagnose. "Pinchy," "sharp," "tight" are stored as your words. The only classification it makes is joint-location versus muscle, because the documents make exactly that distinction and no finer one.
  • It does not invent a fix. If a situation has no rule (Section 08), it says so and logs your decision. It will not reach for "generally sensible" training advice, because that is the thing the documents were written to replace.
  • It does not apply anything. Every proposal waits for a yes. A "yes" is itself a log entry, so the record shows what the plan was and what you actually did.
  • It does not plan around unknowns. It does not reshuffle the week on a guess that Thursday might be busy. It acts when a skip or a week-shape note is logged, not before.
  • It does not coach the lift. "Last rep looks like the first" is a video check; Block 1 week 2's technique bank is the mechanism for that. "Grindy" is logged as a note, not scored.
  • It does not touch nutrition, body weight, mood or schedule. Body weight is explicitly keyed to nothing in the base. Everything else is out of scope and is logged as a note if mentioned.
  • It does not fire on a single data point except where the documents do: a joint-kind ache starts a clock (one point), a tier pass is one session (one point), but stalls need two sessions and the sleep week needs seven nights.
  • It does not run multi-week analysis beyond the specific comparisons named in the documents: week 12 versus weeks 1–2, three consecutive tapes, two consecutive blocks.
When a note matches nothing

The note is saved verbatim with its date, and the reply is: "Logged as a note, word for word. Nothing in the program keys off this, so no change proposed." If the note is partly recognised (a body part with no side, a lift with no numbers), the reply is one question, and the entry sits as pending until it is answered. Never two questions, never a guess. If you do not answer, it stays a note.

08Gaps the documents leave open

The self-check for this page was: does every adjustment trace to a quotable rule? Everything in Section 04 does. But making the rules computable exposed six places where the documents use a word without defining it, or state a rule without covering a case the log will certainly produce. The documents themselves are still closed — nothing below is a new training rule invented to paper over the gap. It is Meciah's own decision, made directly on 2026-11-22 when each gap was brought to him as a one-sentence question, on how this logging layer should read an undefined word. If a future version of the base, Block 1 or Mobility documents ever states its own answer, that document wins and this page has a bug.

  1. "Stalled" is never defined. The base says "a two-session stall on two or more lifts"; Block 1 says "if two lifts have stalled for two sessions each." Neither says what a stalled session is under double progression. Decided 2026-11-22: a session at the same load where total working-set reps did not exceed the previous session's. Two consecutive such sessions is a stall — the most literal reading of "add reps session to session" failing, confirmed over a stricter reading (reps actually falling) and a looser one (any single set missing a rep).
  2. Which seven nights make a "sleep week." "A week averaging under 7 hours" could be Mon–Sun of the training week, which is unknowable on a Wednesday, or the trailing seven nights. Decided 2026-11-22: trailing seven nights ending last night, confirmed over a Mon–Sun calendar window, so the rule can be evaluated on the morning of a tier test. Fewer than five logged nights: the system says it cannot evaluate and tests nothing new, which is the Priority 1 default.
  3. A 48-hour event that matures late in the week. "For that week" is clear when the clock confirms on a Wednesday. When an ache starts Thursday and is confirmed Saturday, "that week" is already over, and the documents do not say whether the drop carries into Monday. Decided 2026-11-22: it carries. The drop continues into the following week's first session on that movement until the ache is confirmed gone — chosen over resetting the clock on Monday, on Priority-1 grounds: an ache that was never confirmed closed should not silently lapse just because the calendar page turned.
  4. A forced loss of Wednesday or a pull day. The four-slot rule says which day to drop when you choose; it says nothing about a week where Thursday is simply gone. "Do not cram" rules out moving it. Decided 2026-11-22: the system logs the lost day as a four-slot week and proposes nothing — no question asked, no attempt to recover the lost pattern. Chosen specifically to avoid inventing a cram-or-move exception the documents rule out; what (if anything) to do about it is Meciah's call each time, not a standing rule.
  5. "One tier back" for a loaded lift. The override offers "one tier, or out of the session." Barbell lifts have no tiers. Decided 2026-11-22: the lift runs at 80% of its current working load for the week, same reps, rather than being dropped entirely — this is the real numeric equivalent of "one tier back" the override lacked. See the updated B5 in Section 04.
  6. Which lifts count toward "two or more lifts." Isolation work and calves also run double progression. Decided 2026-11-22: the base §08 compound list only (bench/floor press, overhead press, squat, deadlift, RDL, rows, weighted pull-ups and chin-ups), confirmed over including calves and isolation work, because that compound list is the exact sentence the deload rule appears in. Calves and lateral raises stall without consequence, by design.

Two smaller notes from the same check. The reply for D1 (thigh falling with the waist) names a fifth squat set and a fourth RDL set; the base says only "volume goes up," so the specific sets are the system's smallest-step suggestion and are labelled that way in the reply. And P4's ordering (pistol to 2 sets, then a fifth squat set) joins a Mobility sentence to a base sentence that were written separately; both are quoted, but the sequence between them is this page's reading.
