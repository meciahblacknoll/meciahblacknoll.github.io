Bedroom gym · Phase 1 · built, not bought

A training skeleton, not a workout

This is the governing logic and a first real weekly structure — built around your stated goals, returning-lifter baseline, and the rule that joint health wins every tie-break. It's deliberately not yet anatomy-tailored in the fullest sense; the open items at the bottom are what's still missing for that.

The three goals, as stated

Aesthetic
  • Greek-statue proportions — balanced, built, not just big
  • No skinny legs — legs get real direct volume, not an afterthought
  • No fixed numeric target; this gets more specific once there's a visible baseline
Skill / strength
  • Full front split, full side split
  • Full planche
  • 3+ strict handstand push-ups
Health
  • Joints feel protected, safe, good — not sacrificed for the other two
  • Connective tissue gets stronger over years, never just "good enough for now"
  • This goal wins any conflict with the other two, by your own call

Why it's structured this way

01
Two different overload mechanisms, running at once. Barbell/dumbbell lifts progress the normal way: hit the top of your rep range, add weight next time. Bodyweight skill lines (planche, handstand push-ups, splits) don't have that dial — they progress through leverage tiers instead. The rule that governs when you're allowed to move to a harder tier: 3-4 sets of 8-12 clean reps, or a clean 30-second hold, at the current tier, before advancing. This is the standard from Steven Low's Overcoming Gravity, the reference text for exactly this kind of progression.
02
Joint health is a pacing rule, not a separate workout. Tendons and ligaments adapt 2-3× slower than muscle. Practically: every session opens with a few minutes of joint-specific CARs (controlled articular rotations) for whatever you're about to load, and the progression rule above is never skipped for being "ready early." A new ache that lingers past 48 hours knocks that movement back a tier that week — no exceptions. That's what "joint health wins the tie-break" actually means in practice, not just as a stated value.
03
Movement patterns, not muscle groups, organize the week. Every pattern — horizontal push/pull, vertical push/pull, squat, hinge, core — gets hit roughly twice a week, which is the frequency sweet spot in the hypertrophy literature and also what keeps a repeating week from quietly neglecting legs (direct cause of "skinny legs").
04
Flexibility doesn't fit the same weekly rhythm as strength. Real split progress comes from near-daily stretch exposure (a time-under-stretch adaptation), not a once-a-week session. So split work shows up in small doses every day as a baseline layer underneath the whole week, with one dedicated longer block on its own leg day.
05
Skills get practiced 2-3×/week, not once. Planche and handstand push-up work appear on two different days each (once as the main focus, once as a lighter second touch), because skill acquisition responds better to frequent, sub-maximal practice than to one hard weekly session.

The repeating week

5 days, repeated indefinitely, progressing in place rather than changing shape — matches wanting something you run for years, not something you get bored of and replace.

Day 1 · Upper A
Horizontal push / pull
  1. Barbell bench press — 4×6-10
  2. Barbell or DB row — 4×8-12
  3. Incline DB press — 3×10-12
  4. Band face pulls — 3×15-20
  5. Hollow body hold — 3×20-30s
shoulder CARs + wrist prep first
Day 2 · Lower A
Squat emphasis
  1. Barbell back squat (goblet squat until rack is sorted) — 4×6-10
  2. Romanian deadlift — 3×8-12
  3. Walking lunges — 3×12/leg
  4. Calf raises — 3×15-20
  5. Front split progression hold — 3×45-60s/side
hip CARs + 90/90 first
Day 3 · Skill
Low-fatigue, joint-protective by design
  1. Handstand practice — 5-8×20-40s
  2. Handstand push-up progression — 4×quality reps
  3. Planche progression (tuck lean/hold) — 4×10-20s
  4. Side split progression — 3×45-60s
  5. L-sit progression — 3×max hold
everything sub-maximal — quality over fatigue
Day 4 · Upper B
Vertical push / pull
  1. Overhead press — 4×6-10
  2. Weighted/assisted pull-ups or rows — 4×6-10
  3. Dips (bench or rings) — 3×8-12
  4. DB lateral raise — 3×12-15
  5. Planche progression — 2nd weekly touch, lighter
band pull-aparts + shoulder CARs first
Day 5 · Lower B
Hinge emphasis
  1. Deadlift (conventional or RDL, rack-dependent) — 4×5-8
  2. Front squat or goblet squat — 3×8-12
  3. Bulgarian split squat — 3×10/leg
  4. Calf raises — 3×15-20
  5. Side split progression — 2nd weekly touch, dedicated block
handstand push-up — 2nd weekly touch

Non-negotiable, every single day

5-10 min of joint CARs (shoulders, hips, spine, wrists, ankles) before anything else, and a short split-stretch touch (5 min) even on full rest days. Flexibility is the one adaptation here that genuinely decays without near-daily input — it doesn't fit neatly into a 4-5-day structure the way strength does, so it sits underneath the whole week instead of inside it.

Progression rules — the actual dial

Barbell / dumbbell lifts

Hit the top of the rep range for every set → add the smallest available increment next session. This is the entire mechanism — no separate deload schedule needed at this stage.

Bodyweight skill lines (planche, HSPU, handstand, splits)

Advance to the next progression tier only after 3-4 sets of 8-12 clean reps, or a clean 30-second hold, at the current tier. Never sooner, even if it starts feeling easy early — the connective tissue adapting underneath the movement is slower than the muscle feeling ready.

Joint-health override

Any new ache lingering past 48 hours drops that specific movement back a tier, or out of the session entirely that week. This overrides both of the rules above, every time, no exceptions — this is what makes "joint health wins" a mechanism rather than a slogan.

Still open before this is truly anatomy-tailored

  • Your actual current handstand hold time, split depth (fist-widths from floor), planche-lean tolerance, and HSPU regression tier — these set the real starting rung on each skill line, rather than a generic starting point
  • Any shoulder/hip structural tendencies that make one skill line more natural than another for your build specifically — needs either real testing or just noticing what feels disproportionately easy or hard once this is running
  • Equipment timeline — this assumes a used barbell + plates + some kind of rack show up; goblet squat and dumbbell press substitutes are already written in above as the fallback until then
